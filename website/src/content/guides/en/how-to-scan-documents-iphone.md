You don’t need a scanner to turn a letter, a receipt or a signed form into a clean PDF. Every recent iPhone has a document scanner built into Apple’s own apps. It finds the edges of the page, straightens it, evens out the lighting and saves several pages as one document. This guide covers where to find it, how to get good scans, how to save, sign and share them, and where your scans end up afterwards.

## Which app to use

The same scanner sits in three Apple apps. Pick the one that matches what you want to do with the scan.

| App | Best for | Saved as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Notes** | A scan with some text around it, quick reference | Embedded in a note |
| **Files** | A standalone PDF to email, upload or archive | PDF in a folder you choose |
| **Preview** (iOS 26) | Scanning, reading and marking up PDFs in one place | PDF, also visible in Files |
| **Camera** | Copying a line of text with Live Text | A photo in your library |

## Scan a document in Notes

1. Open **Notes** and create a new note, or open one you want to add the scan to.
2. Tap the **attachment** button (a paperclip; a camera icon on older iOS versions) and choose **Scan Documents**. You can also tap the **More** button in an open note and choose **Scan**.
3. Point the camera at the page. The scan is taken automatically in Auto mode.
4. Drag the corners if the frame doesn’t fit the page, then tap **Keep Scan**.
5. Scan more pages, or tap **Save** (**Done** on iOS 26). The pages appear as one document in the note.

Tap the scan in the note later to crop, rotate, change the filter, add pages or delete a page.

## Scan a document in Files

Files is the most direct route to a PDF.

1. Open **Files** and go to the **Browse** tab.
2. Tap the **More** button (three dots) at the top and choose **Scan Documents**.
3. Scan the pages the same way as in Notes, then tap **Save** (**Done** on iOS 26).
4. Choose where to save: a folder in **iCloud Drive** or **On My iPhone**. Tap **Save**.

The result is a PDF named something like “Scanned Document”. Touch and hold it and choose **Rename** to give it a name you will recognise later.

## Scan with Preview on iOS 26

iOS 26 added Preview, the PDF and image viewer Mac users know. Open **Preview** and tap **Scan Documents** on its main screen, or the add button if a file is already open. The scanner works the same way, and the PDF is saved where Preview keeps its files, which you can also browse in the Files app.

## Tips for a clean scan

- **Auto or Manual.** Auto mode captures as soon as the page is steady and detected. If it keeps firing too early or not at all, switch to **Manual** at the top of the scanner and tap the shutter, or press a volume button.
- **Contrast helps.** A white page on a dark table is detected faster than white on white.
- **Light without glare.** Even daylight works best. Use the flash option only if the room is dark, and tilt glossy paper away from lamps.
- **Filters.** Choose colour, greyscale, black and white or photo. Black and white makes text crisp and keeps files small; colour is better for stamps, signatures and ID cards. On iOS 26 the flash and filter options sit in the scanner’s options while you capture, and you can change the filter afterwards too.
- **Fix it later.** Opening a saved scan lets you re-crop, rotate or retake a single page without redoing the whole document.

## Save, share and sign a scan

### Save as PDF

Files and Preview already save PDFs. For a scan in Notes, open the note, tap the scan, tap the **Share** button and choose **Save to Files**. You can also send it straight from the share sheet to Mail, Messages or another app.

### Sign or fill in a scan

1. Open the scan in Notes, or the PDF in Files or Preview.
2. Tap the **Markup** button (a pen tip).
3. Tap the **add** button (+) and choose **Signature**. Use a saved signature or create a new one with your finger.
4. Drag and resize the signature into place and tap **Done**.

Markup also adds text boxes, so you can fill in a form you scanned and send it back without printing.

### Copy text from a scan

Live Text recognises text in photos and in the Camera viewfinder, so you can select, copy or translate it. To copy a single line without saving anything, open the **Camera**, point it at the text and tap the **Live Text** button that appears in the corner. If you don’t see it, check that Live Text is turned on in **Settings › General › Language & Region**.

## Where are scanned documents saved?

This is the question that trips people up, because each app saves somewhere different.

- **Notes scans** live inside the note. If the note is in your **iCloud** account, it syncs to your other devices and to Apple’s servers. If it is in an **On My iPhone** account, it stays on the device. The folder list in Notes shows which account a note belongs to.
- **Files scans** go to the folder you picked when saving. **iCloud Drive** syncs to Apple’s servers and your other devices; **On My iPhone** keeps the file local. If you can’t find a scan, open the **Recents** tab in Files or search for “Scanned”.
- **Preview scans** are PDFs in Preview’s folder, which you can also open from Files.
- **Camera photos** of a document go to your Photos library and, if iCloud Photos is on, into iCloud.

Files in iCloud Drive and notes in iCloud are encrypted by Apple, but Apple holds the keys for most categories unless you turn on [Advanced Data Protection](id:icloud-advanced-data-protection-explained). Deleted scans go to **Recently Deleted** in Files or Notes and can usually be recovered for up to 30 days.

## Keeping sensitive scans private

For a supermarket receipt none of this matters much. For a passport page, a medical letter or a tax return, think about where the scan will live before you tap Save. Choosing On My iPhone instead of iCloud keeps it off Apple’s servers, a locked note adds a separate password, and deleting leftovers from Recently Deleted avoids stray copies.
