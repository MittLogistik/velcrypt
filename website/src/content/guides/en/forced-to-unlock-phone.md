Being made to unlock your phone is frightening, whether it is a partner who insists on “just checking”, a stranger on the street, or someone who grabs your phone and won’t give it back. This guide is about getting through that moment safely and preparing so that, if it happens, less is at stake.

It is general information, not legal or professional advice.

## First: your safety comes before your data

If refusing could get you hurt, unlock the phone. That is not failure. Files can be protected in advance, and most of the protection in this guide only works if you prepare before the moment. In the moment, the priority is getting out of it safely.

If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number.

## Three common situations

### A controlling or abusive partner

A partner who demands to see your phone, checks your messages or gets angry when you want privacy may be showing controlling behaviour, especially if it happens again and again. Protecting your files can help, but it doesn’t solve the underlying problem.

- **Talk to someone.** Domestic abuse support services can help confidentially, including with planning how to stay safe. Search for services in your country, ideally from a device the other person can’t check.
- **Check who has access.** On iOS 16 and later, Settings › Privacy & Security › Safety Check shows who you share location, photos and accounts with. [After a breakup](id:after-a-breakup) covers it step by step.
- **Think about sudden changes.** If a partner is used to seeing everything, a phone that suddenly looks different may raise questions. Change things at a pace that feels safe.
- **Keep evidence safe.** If you are documenting harassment, keep a second copy somewhere the other person can’t reach. [Documenting harassment evidence](id:documenting-harassment-evidence) explains how. Note that a duress pattern would delete any vault other than the decoy, including one that holds evidence, so back it up separately.

### Robbery or a threat

A thief usually wants the phone, money or accounts, and wants them quickly. Hand over what they ask for if it keeps you safe. Afterwards:

- Use **Find My** from another device to mark the phone as lost, and erase it remotely if needed.
- Change your Apple Account password and the passwords of important accounts, starting with email and banking.
- Contact your bank and mobile carrier, and report the theft to the police.

### Snooping by a friend, relative or colleague

Not every demand is dangerous. Sometimes it is just someone who doesn’t respect boundaries. A calm “That’s private” is often enough. If you want to show one photo without exposing the rest, [Guided Access](id:guided-access-show-one-photo) can lock the screen to a single app.

## Prepare before it happens

### Keep private files out of the camera roll

The most common exposure is not a hack but someone scrolling. Move private photos, videos and documents into an encrypted vault, then [delete the originals](id:delete-original-photos-after-import) and empty Recently Deleted. A file that isn’t in Photos can’t be found by scrolling through Photos.

### Make it hard to unlock without you

- **Use a strong device passcode** of six digits or more, and don’t share it.
- **Learn the quick Face ID lockout.** On iPhone 8 and later, press and hold the side button and either volume button until the power-off/SOS screen appears, then tap Cancel. Face ID and Touch ID stay off until the passcode is entered. Depending on your settings, pressing the side button repeatedly can call emergency services, so practise the exact gesture beforehand.
- **Velcrypt has no Face ID unlock by design**, so nobody can open a vault by holding the phone up to your face. A pattern exists only in your memory. [Pattern vs PIN vs Face ID](id:pattern-lock-vs-pin-vs-face-id) explains the trade-off.

### Set up a decoy vault

A [decoy vault](id:what-is-a-decoy-vault) is a second vault with harmless, believable content: receipts, ID scans, gift ideas. If someone demands to see what is in the app, you can open the decoy. In Velcrypt, every pattern opens its own vault and there is no list of vaults, so nothing on screen shows that another one exists. A second vault needs Premium.

### Consider a duress pattern, carefully

If you expect to be pressed again later, a decoy alone may not be enough. Velcrypt’s [duress pattern](id:duress-pattern), part of Premium, goes further: drawing it on the lock screen opens your decoy vault and silently, permanently deletes every other vault on the iPhone. The unlock looks and takes the same time as normal, and the app never shows whether a duress pattern is set.

Before you rely on it:

- **It is irreversible.** Deleted vaults can only be restored from an encrypted backup file you made earlier (Settings → Backup, free). Store that file somewhere the other person can’t reach.
- **It protects files, not you.** If someone later notices that files they had seen before are gone, they may react badly. Only use it if you judge it safe.
- **It only reaches Velcrypt.** Copies in Photos, iCloud or chats stay where they are.

[How to set up a decoy vault and duress pattern](id:set-up-decoy-vault-and-duress-pattern) walks through the setup, including the backup.

## In the moment

1. **Stay as calm as you can.** Slow breathing helps. Hesitation and panic draw attention.
2. **Decide quickly whether refusing is safe.** If it isn’t, comply.
3. **If you open Velcrypt, open the vault you prepared for this**, without hesitating. Practising beforehand makes this easier.
4. **Don’t argue about what is or isn’t on the phone.** You don’t owe anyone a tour, and arguing can escalate things.
5. **Get away safely afterwards** and, if needed, contact someone you trust.

## Police, border officials and other legal demands

Everything above is about personal safety. A lawful demand from police, border officials or a court is different. Laws on compelled unlocking differ by country, and deleting data in response to a legal demand can itself be an offence, such as destroying evidence or obstruction. Don’t use a duress pattern, or any other deletion, in that situation.

Stay polite, ask what you are required to do, and seek legal advice. For travel, see [phone privacy at the border](id:phone-privacy-border-crossing).

## When to get help

Contact local support services, someone you trust, or emergency services if:

- someone repeatedly demands access to your phone, or punishes you for privacy;
- you are afraid of how a person will react if you say no;
- you have been threatened, followed or hurt.

A vault can keep files private. It can’t make a situation safe on its own, and you deserve support with the rest.
