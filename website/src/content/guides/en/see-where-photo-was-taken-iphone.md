Your iPhone quietly records where almost every photo was taken. That makes it easy to find holiday pictures by place, but it also means a photo you send can reveal your home, your workplace or where you were on a given day. Here is how to see that location for yourself, what else the Photos app shows, and how to keep the location to yourself when you share.

## See where a single photo was taken

1. Open the **Photos** app and tap the photo.
2. Swipe up, or tap the **Info** (ⓘ) button.
3. If the photo has location data, a small map and an address appear. Tap the map or the address link to open the place in more detail.
4. To see other photos taken in the same spot, tap **Show Nearby Photos**.

If you see **Add a location** instead of a map, the photo has no location stored. Swipe down or tap ⓘ again to close the panel.

## See all your photos on a map

The Photos app can show your whole library on a map, grouped by place. On iOS 26 and later:

1. Open **Photos** and tap **Library**.
2. Tap the **Sort and Filter** button, then tap **Show Map**.
3. Pinch to zoom, and tap a thumbnail to see every photo taken at that location.

You can also tap **Collections**, then the **Map** collection under Pinned. On iOS 18 it sits in Pinned Collections as **Map**. Older versions of iOS, and Photos on a Mac, call it the **Places** album. Only photos and videos with embedded GPS data appear on the map, which makes it a quick way to check how much of your library carries a location.

## What else the Info panel shows

Swiping up shows more than the place. Depending on the photo, you see:

| Detail | What it tells someone |
|---|---|
| Location (map and address) | Where the photo was taken, often to within a few metres |
| Date and time | When you were there |
| Device | Which iPhone model took it |
| Lens and camera settings | Front or back camera, focal length, shutter speed, ISO, file size |
| Caption, people and pets | Names and descriptions you or Photos added |

The location, date, device and camera details are stored inside the image file as metadata (mostly EXIF). Captions and people names live in your Photos library and are not normally part of the shared file. When you share an original photo, the embedded metadata can go with it.

## Why your photos have a location at all

Location is only added if the **Camera** app has access to Location Services. Apple explains that the Camera then uses GPS, Wi-Fi, cellular and Bluetooth information to work out the coordinates and embeds them in each photo and video. Most people allow this when the Camera first asks, so most libraries are full of locations.

## Turn location for the camera off (or back on)

To stop new photos recording where they were taken:

1. Open **Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services**.
2. Tap **Camera**.
3. Choose **Never**.

To turn it back on, choose **While Using the App** in the same place, and make sure Location Services itself is switched on at the top of that screen. If you want rough location without pinpoint accuracy, leave Camera on and turn off **Precise Location** instead.

Turning it off only affects new photos. Everything already in your library keeps its location until you remove it.

## Remove or change the location on a photo

If a particular photo shouldn’t carry a location:

1. Open the photo and tap the **More** (•••) button.
2. Tap **Adjust Location**. On some versions you first tap **Adjust Info**.
3. Tap **No Location**, or type a different place and pick it from the list.

You can also swipe up and tap **Adjust** next to the location. To change several photos at once, tap **Select** in your library, choose the photos and follow the same steps. If you change your mind, the same menu has **Revert** to restore the original location. The [full metadata removal guide](id:remove-photo-metadata-iphone) covers this in more detail.

## Share a photo without its location

You don’t have to edit your library to share safely. The share sheet can drop the location for one share:

1. Select one or more photos and tap **Share**.
2. Tap **Options** at the top of the share sheet.
3. Turn off **Location**. “Location Not Included” appears above Options.
4. Share as usual with Messages, Mail, AirDrop or another app.

The setting applies to that share only, so build it into your habit whenever you send photos to people you don’t know well, sell something online or post publicly.

## What messaging apps strip or keep

Apps handle metadata differently, and their behaviour can change with updates, so treat this as a rough guide rather than a guarantee: <!-- TODO verify -->

- **AirDrop, Mail and Messages** generally send the photo with its metadata unless you turn off Location in Options.
- **WhatsApp, Signal and Telegram** typically remove location when you send an image as a normal photo, but sending it as a file or document can keep the original metadata.
- **Social networks** usually strip location from the image others can download, although the service itself may read it during upload.

The safe habit is the same everywhere: turn off Location before you share, rather than trusting the receiving app.
