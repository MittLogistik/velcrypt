iCloud Shared Photo Library is one of the nicest things Apple has built for families: one collection of holiday photos, birthdays and school plays that everyone can add to. It is also the place where a screenshot of a bank statement or a private photo can quietly end up in front of your partner, parents or teenagers. This guide explains exactly what the other participants can see and do, how photos get into the library without you tapping anything, and how to keep the photos you don't want to share out of it.

## What an iCloud Shared Photo Library is

A Shared Library is a second photo library that sits next to your own **Personal Library** in the Photos app. Some basics, according to Apple:

- It needs iOS 16.1, iPadOS 16.1 or macOS Ventura or later, with iCloud Photos turned on.
- It holds up to six people: the organizer who set it up and up to five others. You can belong to only one Shared Library at a time.
- The organizer's iCloud storage pays for everything in it. Other participants don't use their own storage.
- When you contribute a photo, it **moves** out of your Personal Library into the Shared Library. It is not a copy.
- You don't need Family Sharing to create one. Children under 13 (the age varies by country) can only create or join one with members of their Family Sharing group.

## What the other participants can see and do

This is the part most people underestimate. A Shared Library is not "view only" for anyone. Apple states that all participants can add, edit and delete content.

| Action on an item in the Shared Library | Any participant | Only the contributor | Only the organizer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| See the photo or video, including its date, location and captions | Yes | | |
| Edit, crop, favourite, add captions or change the date | Yes, and everyone sees the change | | |
| Delete it (it goes to Recently Deleted) | Yes | | |
| Delete it permanently from Recently Deleted | | Yes | |
| Move it back to a Personal Library | | Yes | |
| Add or remove participants, delete the library | | | Yes |

Shared Library content also shows up in everyone's Memories, Featured Photos and the Photos widget. So a photo you moved there can appear on someone else's Home Screen without them looking for it.

What participants **cannot** see is your Personal Library. If your partner sees your private photos on the family iPad, the cause is usually a device signed in to your Apple Account, not the Shared Library. Our guide to [photos showing up on the family iPad](id:family-shared-icloud) untangles that case.

## How photos get into the Shared Library

There are five routes, and only the first one requires a deliberate tap on each photo.

1. **Manual moves.** Select photos in Photos, tap the More button (•••) and choose **Move to Shared Library**. During setup you can also move past photos in bulk, for example everything since a date or everything with certain people in it.
2. **The Camera's Shared Library button.** Once you've joined, the Camera shows a Shared Library button at the top of the screen. When it is on, new photos and videos go straight to the Shared Library. When it is off, they go to your Personal Library.
3. **Share Automatically.** With this option, the Camera adds photos to the Shared Library automatically when you are near other participants. Nearby detection relies on participants being signed in to their Apple Account with Bluetooth turned on.
4. **Share When At Home.** Photos and videos you take at home are added to the Shared Library automatically.
5. **Sharing suggestions.** Photos suggests pictures you might want to add, such as photos taken when participants were nearby or photos of people you chose during setup, and sends a notification to review them. They are listed under **For Your Shared Library**. Suggestions don't move anything until you accept them, but it's easy to accept a batch without checking every photo.

## How to keep private photos out

You don't have to leave the library to keep it clean. Change these settings instead:

1. Go to **Settings › Apps › Photos › Shared Library** (on iOS 17 and earlier, **Settings › Photos › Shared Library**). In the Photos app you can reach the same screen via Collections, then your account in the top-right corner, then Shared Library.
2. Tap **Sharing from Camera**. Choose **Share Manually** so the Camera only saves to the Shared Library when you switch it on yourself, or turn Sharing from Camera off completely. Make sure **Share When At Home** is off too.
3. Turn off **Shared Library Suggestions** if you don't want regular prompts to share more.
4. Before taking a private photo, glance at the Shared Library button in the Camera and make sure it is off.
5. Check what is already shared. Switch the library view in Photos to **Shared Library** to see only shared items; in the Both Libraries view, shared items carry a small badge in the corner of the thumbnail. Where this view option sits has moved between iOS versions; on iOS 26, tap Library and then the Sort and Filter button.

## Moving photos back to your Personal Library

Open a photo or video in the Shared Library, tap the More button (•••) and choose **Move to Personal Library**. You can also select several items first. After the move, other participants can no longer see them.

Two limits matter. First, you can only move items **you** contributed; a photo of you that someone else added stays in the Shared Library unless they move it or delete it. Second, moving a photo back doesn't undo what already happened: a participant may have saved it, sent it in Messages or exported it while it was shared. Treat anything that was in the Shared Library as seen.

## Hidden photos and the Shared Library

Hiding is not a way to keep a photo private inside a Shared Library. According to Apple, if you hide a photo that is in your Shared Library, it moves to the Hidden album **for all participants**, where they can view it or unhide it. The Hidden album is locked with Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode by default, but that is each participant's own unlock, not yours.

The safe order is: move the photo to your Personal Library first, then hide it. Keep in mind that hidden photos in your Personal Library still sync to every device signed in to your Apple Account; see [are hidden photos saved to iCloud?](id:are-hidden-photos-saved-to-icloud)

## Leaving or deleting the Shared Library

In Photos, tap Collections, then your account in the top-right corner, then **Manage**:

- **Leave Shared Library** (participants): you choose whether to copy everything from the Shared Library into your Personal Library or only what you contributed.
- **Delete Shared Library** (organizer only): every participant is notified. People who were in it for more than seven days can copy everything; those who joined less than seven days ago get only their own contributions.

The organizer can also remove a single participant. Note that leaving protects your future photos, not past ones: the other participants can keep copies of everything you shared, including your contributions.

## Shared Library, Shared Albums and Family Sharing compared

These three features are often confused, and they behave very differently.

| | iCloud Shared Photo Library | Shared Albums | Family Sharing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A second full library for up to 6 people | Individual albums you share with chosen people | A group for purchases, subscriptions and iCloud+ storage |
| Shares photos automatically | Can, from the Camera and at home | No, you add photos yourself | No |
| Who can edit and delete | Every participant | The owner; subscribers can add if allowed | Not applicable |
| End-to-end encrypted with Advanced Data Protection | Yes, if every participant has it on | No, never | Each person's own library follows their own setting |

## What about encryption?

iCloud Photos is encrypted, but under the default Standard Data Protection Apple holds the keys. With Advanced Data Protection, Apple says shared content stays end-to-end encrypted only **as long as all participants** have it enabled, so one relative without it is enough to lose that protection for the Shared Library. Shared Albums are not covered at all. Our guide to [iCloud Advanced Data Protection](id:icloud-advanced-data-protection-explained) explains how to turn it on and what you give up.

If you're searching for "encrypted family photos", that is the honest answer: the closest Apple gets is a Shared Library where every participant has turned on Advanced Data Protection. Encryption protects photos on Apple's servers. It does nothing about the five other people who are meant to see them.

## Where a separate encrypted vault fits

A Shared Library is built for sharing. For the photos you never want shared, the more reliable approach is to keep them out of the Photos library entirely.

Velcrypt is an iPhone vault for exactly that. Photos, videos and documents you import are encrypted on your iPhone with AES-256-GCM and opened with a pattern drawn on a 5×5 grid, not with the device passcode or Face ID. There is [no account, no server and no sync](id:offline-no-account), and vault data is excluded from iCloud and computer backups, so nothing in a vault can appear in a Shared Library, a shared album or on a family iPad. Each pattern opens its own vault, so you can keep a harmless decoy vault too.

Importing creates an encrypted copy; it doesn't remove the original. Delete the original from Photos afterwards and empty Recently Deleted. If the photo was in the Shared Library, move it to your Personal Library first, or delete it there, and remember that only the contributor can permanently remove it from Recently Deleted.

The honest trade-offs: a vault isn't for sharing, it only exists on your iPhone, and a forgotten pattern cannot be recovered. To protect against a lost phone, create an encrypted backup file in Settings → Backup and keep it somewhere safe. Exporting a photo from the vault creates a normal, unencrypted copy again.

## A quick checklist

- Set Sharing from Camera to Share Manually, or turn it off.
- Turn off Share When At Home and Shared Library Suggestions if you don't need them.
- Check the Camera's Shared Library button before private photos.
- Move your private items back with Move to Personal Library, then hide or vault them.
- Never rely on hiding a photo while it is still in the Shared Library.
- Keep anything that must never be shared outside Photos, in an encrypted on-device vault.
