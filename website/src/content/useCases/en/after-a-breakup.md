Breakups are hard enough without wondering who can still see your photos. Over a relationship, access quietly piles up: a passcode they watched you type, a shared album of trips, a Shared Library set up for the two of you, maybe a fingerprint added to your phone. None of it was a mistake at the time. Now is simply the moment to tidy it up, one step at a time.

If you feel unsafe, or your ex is threatening you, put your safety first and reach out to someone you trust or a local support organisation. The steps below can be done quietly, but changing access can be noticed — think about timing if that matters.

## What can still expose your photos

- **A known passcode.** It unlocks your iPhone, the Hidden album (iOS 16 and later), notes locked with the passcode, and more. See [is the Hidden album private?](id:is-iphone-hidden-album-private)
- **A Face ID alternate appearance or extra fingerprint** they set up on your device.
- **Shared albums** you created together or subscribed to.
- **An iCloud Shared Photo Library** that may still be receiving photos automatically from your camera.
- **A shared Apple Account,** or a device of theirs still signed in to yours.
- **Location and other sharing,** such as Find My, which isn’t about photos but tells someone where they were taken.

## A step-by-step checklist

1. **Change your device passcode.** Settings › Face ID & Passcode (or Touch ID & Passcode) › Change Passcode. Choose one they couldn’t guess.
2. **Reset biometrics if needed.** In the same menu, reset Face ID’s alternate appearance or remove fingerprints you didn’t add yourself.
3. **Run Safety Check.** Go to Settings › Privacy & Security › Safety Check (iOS 16 and later). Use “Manage Sharing & Access” to review people and apps one by one, or Emergency Reset to stop all sharing quickly.
4. **Review shared albums.** In Photos, open each shared album and remove your ex, or unsubscribe from theirs.
5. **Leave or edit the Shared Library.** In the Photos settings under Shared Library, leave the library or remove participants if you’re the organiser. Check where your Camera is saving new photos.
6. **Secure your Apple Account.** Change your Apple Account password, then review the list of devices signed in and remove any you don’t recognise.
7. **Check other apps.** Messaging apps, cloud drives and social accounts may have linked devices or shared folders too.

## Where to keep private photos now

Some photos you’ll want to keep — memories, screenshots of messages, documents from the relationship — without them sitting in a camera roll someone else might glimpse. Velcrypt is one option for that.

Velcrypt stores photos, videos and documents in encrypted vaults on your iPhone. It doesn’t use your device passcode or Face ID; a vault opens with a [pattern you draw on a 5×5 grid](id:pattern-lock), so a passcode your ex once knew doesn’t help them. [Each pattern opens its own vault](id:hidden-vaults), and an unknown pattern just opens an empty one with no error, so nothing reveals what’s stored.

After importing, [delete the originals](id:delete-original-photos-after-import) from Photos and empty Recently Deleted — otherwise copies remain in your library and, with iCloud Photos on, on your other devices. If intimate photos are part of this, [this guide](id:store-intimate-photos-safely-iphone) goes into more detail.

## Honest limits

- Nothing on your phone can remove copies your ex already has: photos you sent, saved from a shared album, or in their backups.
- A vault doesn’t help if someone watches you draw your pattern, or if the phone is taken while the vault is open.
- Vault data isn’t included in iCloud or computer backups. If you forget your pattern, the vault can’t be recovered; if you lose the phone, only an encrypted backup you made in Settings → Backup can bring it back. Keep a backup or a second copy of anything irreplaceable somewhere safe.
- The app icon is visible on your Home Screen unless you use iOS 18’s option to hide apps.

## If it goes further

If an ex is sharing or threatening to share your private images, monitoring you or harassing you, it’s not something you have to handle alone. Our page on [documenting harassment evidence](id:documenting-harassment-evidence) covers keeping records safely, and local support organisations and the police in many places can help.

Taking back control of your phone won’t fix a broken heart, but it can make the next few weeks feel a little more like your own.
