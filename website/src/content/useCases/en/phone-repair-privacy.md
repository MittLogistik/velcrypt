A cracked screen or a battery that dies at 40% is stressful enough. Then comes the uncomfortable thought: someone you’ve never met will be holding a device that contains your whole life. A little preparation makes the handover a lot calmer.

## What a technician could actually access

Most repair technicians are professionals who have no interest in your photos. Still, it helps to know what’s possible:

- **With your passcode:** everything. Photos, the Hidden album (it unlocks with the passcode on iOS 16 and later), Files, Mail, Messages and notes locked with the device passcode. See [is the Hidden album private?](id:is-iphone-hidden-album-private)
- **Without your passcode:** very little on a locked, working iPhone. iOS encrypts user data and ties it to your passcode, so files aren’t readable without unlocking.
- **During diagnostics:** some repairs require the phone to boot and run tests. Providers may ask for your passcode to confirm a fix works.
- **If the device is replaced or erased:** your data only comes back from a backup.

## Before you hand it over

1. **Make a full backup.** Use iCloud Backup or a computer backup. Confirm it finished.
2. **Follow the provider’s Find My instructions.** Recent iOS versions support a repair state that lets the device stay linked to Find My during service, while older flows asked you to switch it off. Ask your provider which applies, or check [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/).
3. **Ask whether your passcode is really needed.** Many hardware repairs don’t require it.
4. **Move sensitive items out of easy reach.** Scans of IDs, medical photos and intimate pictures shouldn’t sit in the Photos library during a repair.
5. **Sign out of anything you can’t afford to have opened,** such as banking or work apps, if your provider needs the passcode.
6. **Keep a way to log in elsewhere.** If two-factor codes arrive on this phone, make sure you have another trusted device or recovery codes while it’s away.

## After you get it back

- Change your device passcode if you shared it.
- Check Settings › Privacy & Security › Safety Check (iOS 16 and later) to review which devices and apps have access.
- Make sure Find My is on and your Apple Account shows only your devices.

## How Velcrypt helps

Velcrypt keeps photos, videos and documents in encrypted vaults on your iPhone. The vault doesn’t open with your device passcode or Face ID; it needs the [pattern you draw on a 5×5 grid](id:pattern-lock). So even if a technician has your passcode and opens Velcrypt, drawing anything other than your pattern just shows an empty vault — no error, no hint that other vaults exist.

Imported photos are copies, so remember to [delete the originals](id:delete-original-photos-after-import) and empty Recently Deleted before the repair. Otherwise they’re still in Photos.

## The honest trade-off: backups

Here’s what you need to weigh carefully. In the first version, Velcrypt vault data is **excluded from iCloud and computer backups**. That’s deliberate — nothing leaves your device — but it means:

- If the repair ends with an erased or replaced iPhone, your vault cannot be restored, by you or by Velcrypt.
- An encrypted backup and recovery option is planned, but not available yet.

So for anything irreplaceable, keep a second copy somewhere you trust before the repair — for example an encrypted drive you control. The same applies if you ever [forget your pattern](id:forgot-vault-pattern). Read more about the design choices on the [security page](id:security).

## Other limits to keep in mind

- Copies of photos in iCloud Photos, Messages or email are not protected by a vault. Check [whether hidden photos sync to iCloud](id:are-hidden-photos-saved-to-icloud).
- The Velcrypt icon is visible on the Home Screen unless you use iOS 18’s option to hide apps.
- No app protects against malware on a jailbroken or tampered device. If you suspect tampering after a repair, have the phone checked by Apple or an authorised provider.

A repair shouldn’t require trusting a stranger with everything. Back up, move the sensitive things out of the obvious places, and you can hand your phone over with a clear head.
