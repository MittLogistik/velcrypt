Handing your phone to a child for ten minutes of cartoons is one of the most ordinary things a parent does. Then a small thumb swipes out of the video, the Photos app opens, and suddenly they’re scrolling past your medical scans, a screenshot of your bank details, or a photo you’d simply rather they didn’t see. Nothing dramatic happened — but it’s a moment worth planning for.

## How kids end up in your camera roll

Children rarely go looking for your private photos. They get there by accident, and the iPhone offers several routes:

- **The Photos app itself.** One swipe up to the Home Screen and the Photos icon is right there.
- **Photo pickers inside apps.** Drawing apps, games with avatars and video editors often ask to pick a photo. If an app has full library access, every photo is shown. You can check this in [which apps can see your photos](id:which-apps-can-see-your-photos-iphone).
- **Memories and widgets.** The Photos widget and Memories surface pictures on their own, sometimes ones you’d forgotten about.
- **The share sheet and Messages.** A curious tap can attach a photo to a message or a family chat.

The Hidden album helps a little: on iOS 16 and later it’s locked with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode by default. But if your child knows your passcode — and many do — that lock is no barrier. More on that in [is the iPhone Hidden album private?](id:is-iphone-hidden-album-private)

## Practical steps that work without any app

### Use Guided Access for short sessions

Guided Access is an iOS accessibility feature that locks the iPhone to a single app. It’s the most effective thing you can do before handing your phone over.

1. Go to Settings › Accessibility › Guided Access and turn it on. Set a passcode or allow Face ID to end sessions.
2. Open the game or video app your child wants.
3. Triple-click the side button (or Home button on older iPhones) and tap Start.
4. To end it, triple-click again and authenticate.

You can even disable parts of the screen, such as an in-app “share” button. The same trick works for showing someone one photo, explained in [our Guided Access guide](id:guided-access-show-one-photo).

### Use Screen Time for longer-term rules

Screen Time (Settings › Screen Time) lets you set content ratings, app limits and Downtime. For a phone your child uses often, it’s worth restricting app installs and in-app purchases. Screen Time doesn’t hide photos, though — it controls apps, not what’s inside them.

### Tidy what’s in the library

Move documents like passports or prescriptions out of the camera roll, review which apps have full photo access, and consider giving older children their own device or a child account through Family Sharing rather than your phone.

## How Velcrypt helps

Velcrypt is a private vault for photos, videos and documents that lives entirely on your iPhone. When you import something, it’s copied into an encrypted vault; once you [delete the original](id:delete-original-photos-after-import) (and empty Recently Deleted), it’s no longer in your Photos library, Memories, widgets or any app’s photo picker.

The vault opens by drawing a [pattern on a 5×5 grid](id:pattern-lock), not with your device passcode or Face ID. So even a child who knows your passcode can’t open it. If they open the app and draw something, they simply see an empty vault — there’s no error message and nothing to suggest there’s more inside.

[Auto-lock](id:auto-lock) closes the vault as soon as the app goes to the background, and its contents are hidden in the app switcher. If you’re looking at a document and hand the phone over, switching away locks it.

## Honest limits

- A vault doesn’t stop a child from seeing anything that’s still in Photos, Messages or other apps. Only what you move into it is protected.
- If your child watches you draw your pattern, they may remember it. Draw it out of sight, and pick a longer one.
- If you hand over the phone with the vault open and the app in the foreground, the content is visible until the app is backgrounded.
- The Velcrypt icon is visible on your Home Screen. iOS 18 and later let you hide apps behind Face ID in a hidden folder; that’s an iOS feature, not part of Velcrypt.
- In the first version, vault data isn’t included in iCloud or computer backups. If the phone is lost or broken, or you forget the pattern, the vault can’t be recovered. Keep copies of irreplaceable photos somewhere safe.

For most families, the best combination is simple: Guided Access every time you hand the phone over, and a vault for the handful of things that should never be one swipe away.
