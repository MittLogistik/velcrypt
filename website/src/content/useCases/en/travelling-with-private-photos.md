Travel puts your phone in more hands and more places than everyday life. You use it as your boarding pass, your wallet, your map and your camera. You charge it in hostels, hand it to a stranger to take a group photo, and occasionally leave it behind in a taxi. A few habits before you leave make all of that much less stressful.

## What actually exposes photos while travelling

- **A lost or stolen phone.** The biggest risk. Thieves sometimes watch you type your passcode before taking the phone, then use it to change your Apple Account settings.
- **Lending your phone.** Someone checks a route or makes a call, then swipes into Photos.
- **Taking a photo for others.** Handing your unlocked camera to a stranger gives them one swipe to your camera roll.
- **Shared rooms and charging spots.** An unlocked phone on a hostel bunk or café table.
- **Border checks.** In some countries officials may ask to inspect devices. That has its own rules and risks, covered in [phone privacy at border crossings](id:phone-privacy-border-crossing).

## Before you leave

1. **Turn on Stolen Device Protection.** Settings › Face ID & Passcode › Stolen Device Protection (iOS 17.3 and later). Away from familiar locations, it requires Face ID or Touch ID — not just your passcode — for actions like changing your Apple Account password, and adds a delay for some changes.
2. **Use a strong passcode.** A six-digit or alphanumeric code is harder to shoulder-surf than four digits. Cover the screen when typing it in public.
3. **Back up the phone.** Make sure your iCloud or computer backup is current.
4. **Check Find My.** Confirm it’s on so you can locate, lock or erase a lost phone.
5. **Move private photos and documents out of Photos.** Passport scans, insurance cards and personal photos shouldn’t be one swipe from the travel snaps. See [medical and ID documents](id:medical-and-id-documents).
6. **Keep emergency copies elsewhere.** A copy of your passport and insurance details somewhere you can reach from another device helps if the phone is gone.

## While you’re away

- **Lend with Guided Access.** Open the app they need, triple-click the side button and start Guided Access. The same approach works for [showing someone a single photo](id:guided-access-show-one-photo).
- **Don’t hand over an unlocked camera.** If a stranger takes your photo, check you’re not passing them a phone that can browse your library — or use a timer and a stable surface.
- **Lock before you put it down.** A quick press of the side button costs nothing.

## How Velcrypt helps

Velcrypt keeps private photos, videos and documents in encrypted vaults stored only on your iPhone. A vault opens with a pattern you draw on a 5×5 grid — not with your passcode or Face ID. If a thief has learned your passcode, that alone doesn’t open your vault. Why a pattern is used instead of biometrics is explained in [pattern vs PIN vs Face ID](id:pattern-lock-vs-pin-vs-face-id).

Every pattern opens its own vault, and an unknown pattern opens an empty one without any error. With Premium, which allows more than one vault, that lets you keep a [decoy vault](id:what-is-a-decoy-vault) with harmless travel photos and keep your private vault behind a different pattern. The app also auto-locks when it goes to the background, and its content is hidden in the app switcher.

There’s no account and nothing is uploaded, so there’s no cloud login to phish or reset while you’re on the road.

## Honest limits

- **No automatic backup.** Vault data is excluded from iCloud and computer backups. If the phone is lost or stolen, so is the vault — unless you saved an encrypted backup file in Settings → Backup before you left. Keep that file, or a second copy of anything irreplaceable, somewhere safe, and remember its backup password.
- **Originals stay in Photos until you delete them.** Importing copies a photo. [Delete the original](id:delete-original-photos-after-import) and empty Recently Deleted.
- **Someone watching you draw** your pattern could learn it.
- **The app icon is visible** on the Home Screen unless you use iOS 18’s option to hide apps.
- **A vault isn’t a legal shield.** At a border, the rules of the country apply. Don’t use the [duress pattern](id:duress-pattern) to delete data when officials lawfully demand access; that can itself be an offence.

Travel light, travel prepared: a locked-down phone means losing it is an inconvenience, not a crisis.
