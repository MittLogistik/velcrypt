If you make things for a living — or for love — your iPhone probably holds work that isn’t ready to be seen. Edits in progress, a shoot under embargo, a client’s proofs, sketches for a commission, or content for a subscription platform. For adult creators especially, a leak isn’t just inconvenient; it can affect income, safety and other people’s privacy. Whatever you create, the principle is the same: you decide when and how your work is seen.

## Where unreleased work leaks on iPhone

- **The camera roll.** Show a friend one photo and they can swipe straight into a client shoot.
- **iCloud Photos sync.** Work photos appear on a family iPad or shared Mac signed in to the same Apple Account. See [family devices and shared iCloud](id:family-shared-icloud).
- **Memories, widgets and search.** iOS surfaces photos automatically, including ones you’d never feature.
- **App photo access.** Editing and social apps with full library access can see everything. Review [which apps can see your photos](id:which-apps-can-see-your-photos-iphone) and switch to limited access where you can.
- **Metadata.** A photo’s EXIF data can include GPS coordinates, date and camera details. Posting a teaser with location data can reveal a studio, a client’s home or your own address.
- **Chats and drafts.** Proofs sent over messaging apps stay on both phones, often in both people’s backups.

## Consent is part of security

When you photograph someone, you hold part of their privacy. Good practice, regardless of genre:

- Agree in writing how images will be stored, who can see them, and when they’ll be deleted.
- Share proofs only with the person or people entitled to see them.
- Don’t keep client material on your phone longer than needed.
- If a client asks you to delete images, delete every copy — including Recently Deleted, chats and backups.

## Practical steps

1. **Separate work from personal.** Move client and unreleased material out of the Photos library. Keep masters in your own archive, not on your phone alone.
2. **Use limited photo access** for apps that don’t need your whole library.
3. **Strip metadata before posting.** Our in-browser [Photo Metadata Remover](id:photo-metadata-remover) shows what a JPEG reveals and saves a clean copy without EXIF or GPS. The [metadata guide](id:remove-photo-metadata-iphone) covers iPhone’s own options.
4. **Export deliberately.** Export only the file you need, to the person who needs it, then remove the exported copy from your device.
5. **Clean up.** After moving files, delete the originals and empty Recently Deleted. See [deleting originals after import](id:delete-original-photos-after-import).

## How Velcrypt helps

Velcrypt stores photos, [videos](id:hide-videos) and documents in encrypted vaults on your iPhone, with no account and nothing uploaded. Inside a vault you can create [albums](id:albums) per project or client, use multi-select, move items between albums, and view photos and videos with zoom and swipe.

Vaults open with a pattern drawn on a 5×5 grid, not your passcode or Face ID. Every pattern opens its own vault, so you can keep client work in one vault and personal photos in another, and a vault for showing a portfolio that contains only what you’re happy for anyone to see. An unknown pattern simply opens an empty vault.

Secure export lets you take a single file out when it’s time to deliver or publish. The app locks automatically when it goes to the background and hides its content in the app switcher, which helps when you switch to a messaging app mid-conversation.

## Honest limits

- **Not an archive.** No cloud sync and no automatic backup: if you forget the pattern, the vault can’t be recovered, and a lost phone can only be recovered from an encrypted backup file you made in Settings → Backup. Keep masters elsewhere.
- **Exports leave the vault.** Once a file is sent, it’s only as private as the recipient’s device.
- **Screen capture by others.** A vault can’t stop someone photographing your screen, or a recipient screenshotting what you send.
- **Check metadata yourself.** Don’t assume any export removes location or camera data; run files through a dedicated tool before publishing.
- **Shoulder-surfing** your pattern, or handing over the phone with a vault open, defeats the lock.

Your work deserves a launch on your terms. Keeping it out of the camera roll until then is a small habit with a big payoff.
