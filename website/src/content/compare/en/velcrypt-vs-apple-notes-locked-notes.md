Locking a note in Apple Notes is one of the quieter privacy tricks on the iPhone. You can drop a few photos or a scanned document into a note, lock it, and it disappears behind your passcode. It’s free and built in. It’s also not really a photo library, and that’s where a dedicated vault like Velcrypt differs.

## How locked notes work

A locked note is a note whose contents are encrypted and hidden until you authenticate. On recent iOS versions you can lock notes with your device passcode, or set a separate Notes password; Face ID or Touch ID can be allowed as a shortcut. The note’s title stays visible in the list, while the body and attachments are hidden. If Notes is synced with iCloud, locked notes sync to your other devices too.

## Where locked notes work well

If you need to keep a copy of a passport scan, a couple of screenshots or a medical letter out of sight, a locked note is quick and reliable. You already have the app, it syncs, and you can reach it on your Mac. For documents specifically, [scanning documents privately](id:scan-documents-privately-iphone) compares the approaches.

## Where a dedicated vault helps

Notes is a writing app first. Once you have dozens or hundreds of images, or videos, scrolling through notes gets awkward. Velcrypt is organised like a photo app: [albums](id:albums) inside each vault, a full-screen viewer with zoom and swipe, multi-select, moving items between albums, and document import from the Files app ([private documents](id:private-documents)).

The other difference is the secret. If you lock notes with your device passcode, anyone who knows the passcode can open them. A separate Notes password fixes that, but there’s still one password for every locked note. Velcrypt’s pattern is independent of your passcode, and each pattern opens a separate vault that doesn’t reveal the others. A pattern the app hasn’t seen opens an empty vault, so you can keep a decoy. See [pattern lock](id:pattern-lock) and [hidden vaults](id:hidden-vaults).

## When Apple Notes may suit you better

- **You only have a few items.** A locked note is simpler than installing anything.
- **You want sync across iPhone, iPad and Mac.** Velcrypt is iPhone-only and doesn’t sync.
- **You want recoverability.** With iCloud, your locked notes survive a lost phone.
- **You like Face ID convenience.** Velcrypt deliberately doesn’t offer it.

## When Velcrypt may suit you better

- **You have a real collection.** Many photos, videos and documents, organised in albums.
- **You want nothing in the cloud.** Vault data stays on the phone and out of backups in v1.
- **You need separate private spaces.** Each pattern is its own vault.
- **Someone else knows your passcode.** The pattern is a separate secret.

## Either way, clean up the originals

Adding a photo to a note or importing it into Velcrypt creates a copy; the original stays in Photos. Delete it and empty Recently Deleted, or it remains there for about 30 days. The steps are in [delete original photos after import](id:delete-original-photos-after-import).
