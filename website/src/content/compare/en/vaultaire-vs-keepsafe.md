Vaultaire and Keepsafe are two of the apps people compare most when they want to hide photos on an iPhone. They solve the same problem from opposite ends. Keepsafe is a long-established vault built around an account, a PIN and an optional cloud. Vaultaire is a newer app built around a drawn pattern, several hidden vaults and no account. This page compares the two as neutrally as we can, using what each one publishes about itself. Because we make Velcrypt, which works much like Vaultaire, we also say where it fits and where it doesn't.

## The short version

- **Choose Vaultaire** if you want no account, a pattern instead of a PIN, hidden vaults, a duress option, and an encrypted iCloud backup with a recovery phrase.
- **Choose Keepsafe** if you want a familiar PIN with Face ID, an account-based cloud, break-in alerts, or an app that also runs on Android.
- **Consider Velcrypt** if you like Vaultaire's pattern model but want nothing in the cloud and no data collection at all, and you accept that there is no automatic backup or recovery phrase.

## Account and cloud: the biggest difference

Keepsafe is built around an account. According to its App Store listing, you sign up with an email address, and the premium plan adds a Private Cloud that can restore your vault on a new phone. That is convenient, and for many people losing photos is a bigger risk than an account.

<!-- TODO verify -->

Vaultaire takes the opposite route on accounts: there is no sign-up, and the pattern itself is the key. It still offers a cloud, though. According to its website, an encrypted backup to your own iCloud is included in the free version and turned on by default, and a 9-word recovery phrase lets you restore a vault if you forget the pattern. The files are encrypted on the phone before they leave it.

Velcrypt goes one step further in the same direction. It has no account and no cloud of any kind, vault data is excluded from iCloud and computer backups, and the app makes no network requests. The trade-off is real: if you lose the phone or forget the pattern, nobody can restore the vault, unless you made an encrypted backup file yourself in Settings → Backup. The details are on [offline, no account](id:offline-no-account).

## Unlocking: pattern versus PIN and Face ID

Keepsafe uses a PIN-style code and, according to its listing, Face ID or Touch ID for convenience.

<!-- TODO verify -->

Vaultaire and Velcrypt both use a pattern drawn through dots on a 5×5 grid, and both leave out Face ID on purpose. The reasoning is the same: a face or a finger can be used without your consent, while a pattern only exists in your memory. A 5×5 grid also has far more possible paths than the 3×3 grid on Android phones. The trade-offs are explained in [pattern lock vs PIN vs Face ID](id:pattern-lock-vs-pin-vs-face-id).

## Decoys and duress

Keepsafe has offered a fake PIN that opens a harmless decoy vault as a premium feature.

<!-- TODO verify -->

In Vaultaire and Velcrypt, decoys are part of the design rather than an add-on. Every pattern opens its own vault, there is no list of vaults, and a pattern the app has never seen opens an empty vault instead of an error. Nothing on the screen proves that other vaults exist; see [hidden vaults](id:hidden-vaults).

Both also have a duress option on their paid plans, but they behave differently:

- **Vaultaire's duress vault**, according to its website, opens the decoy and silently removes access to the other vaults on the device. Its encrypted iCloud backups are kept, so the other vaults can be restored later with their recovery phrases.
- **Velcrypt's [duress pattern](id:duress-pattern)** opens the decoy vault and permanently deletes every other vault on the iPhone. There is no cloud copy to restore from, only a backup file you may have made yourself.

Which behaviour is right depends on your situation. A recoverable wipe protects your photos. A permanent wipe protects you if someone could also force you to restore them.

## Price

Both Vaultaire and Velcrypt charge $1.99 a week, $19.99 a year or $39.99 once for lifetime access in the US store. Vaultaire also has a monthly plan and family plans, and a 7-day trial on the yearly plan; Velcrypt has a 3-day trial. Vaultaire's free version is more generous: up to 3 vaults, 100 photos and 10 videos, against Velcrypt's single vault with up to 50 items. Keepsafe has a free tier and a premium subscription; check its App Store listing for current prices in your country.

## When Vaultaire may suit you better

- **You want a safety net.** An encrypted iCloud backup and a recovery phrase mean a lost phone or a forgotten pattern isn't the end.
- **You use an iPad.** Vaultaire syncs between iPhone and iPad with Pro.
- **You want to share a vault.** Its Pro plan can share a separate, read-only vault with someone else.
- **You want a bigger free version.** Three vaults and more items before you pay.

## When Keepsafe may suit you better

- **You want Face ID.** If quick biometric unlock matters more than the compelled-unlock concern, Keepsafe offers it.
- **You use Android too.** Keepsafe is available on more than one platform.
- **You want break-in alerts.** Keepsafe can photograph someone who enters a wrong PIN, according to its listing.
- **You prefer an account.** Account-based recovery is the most familiar way to get your photos back.

## When Velcrypt may suit you better

- **You want nothing to leave the phone.** No cloud, no sync, no network requests and no analytics.
- **You want an app that collects no data.** Velcrypt's App Store privacy label is "Data Not Collected".
- **You want a permanent duress wipe.** Nobody can restore the deleted vaults, not even you, unless you kept a backup file.
- **You like Vaultaire's pattern model** but not the cloud that comes with it.

Velcrypt is not the right choice if you need a recovery phrase, an iPad app, sharing or Android. The [Velcrypt vs Keepsafe](id:velcrypt-vs-keepsafe) page and the roundup of [private photo vault apps](id:best-private-photo-vault-apps-iphone) go into more detail.

## Switching between them

Whichever app you choose, moving photos follows the same steps: export them from the old app to Photos or Files, import them into the new one, then delete the exported copies and empty Recently Deleted. The last step matters most, because an import usually copies a photo rather than moving it. See [delete original photos after import](id:delete-original-photos-after-import).

## What no vault can fix

No vault app protects you from someone watching you draw your pattern, a phone handed over while the vault is open, or copies that still live in iCloud Photos, chat threads or someone else's phone. A vault is one layer of privacy, not the whole plan.
